Learn to teach yoga, become a certified yoga instructor

Will your teacher training prepare you to teach, or only to hold a certificate?

Will your yoga teacher training prepare you to teach a real class?

Completing a yoga teacher training and becoming a yoga teacher are two different things. When you set out to learn to teach yoga, it is easy to focus on the certificate, the number of hours, the price, the location, or the convenience of studying online. These things matter, yet they do not necessarily tell you what you most need to know: will this training actually prepare you to teach?

Learning yoga for yourself is one thing. Learning to guide a room full of people, explain unfamiliar movements, recognise different physical needs, adapt your instructions and create a safe, welcoming environment is another.

Teaching is a practical skill. It develops through experience, observation, repetition and constructive feedback. You need opportunities to make mistakes, understand what went wrong, try again and gradually find your own voice as a teacher.

My 200-hour foundation yoga teacher training with Raili Maripuu and Katia Major at Thames Wellness Academy consisted of 180 hours of in-studio contact time and 20 hours of self-paced study completed between modules.

For me, this distinction is an important starting point when discussing yoga teacher training. Not all learning happens in the same way. There is a difference between studying material independently and being physically present with experienced teachers, observing their methods, practising your own teaching, asking questions and receiving feedback in real time.

Independent study has its place. It allows you to revisit material, reflect on what you have learned and work through concepts at your own pace. Learning to teach yoga also involves practical skills that benefit from direct interaction, observation and guided practice.

When choosing a teacher training, it is therefore worth looking beyond the headline number of hours and understanding how those hours are actually spent. This is why I believe aspiring yoga teachers should look beyond the certificate and ask deeper questions about the training they are about to undertake. If your aim is to become a certified yoga instructor who can hold a room with care, those questions matter.

I want to share my own experience to explain why.
